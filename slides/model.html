The model: make the wind part of the state

State, 9 dimensions

x = [ px py pz , vx vy vz , wx wy wz ]

Per axis

pt+1 = pt + vt Δt

vt+1 = vt + ( a − k(vt − wt) ) Δt

wt+1 = φ wt + εt

Drag acts on airspeed, not ground speed

The term is (v − w). That is what couples wind into the dynamics and makes it observable — we infer the gust from how the drone is pushed off course.

Gusts are correlated, not white noise

Wind is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with φ = e−Δt/τ. Real gusts persist for seconds. Crucially this stays linear, so the Kalman filter remains exact — no EKF needed.

Assumption: the IMU channel carries thrust acceleration; aerodynamic disturbance is left out of it, so the filter must infer wind rather than read it.

Two design decisions are worth defending in the viva. First, drag depends on airspeed, the velocity relative to the air, which is v minus w. If we had written drag as acting on ground speed, the wind would never enter the dynamics and would be completely unobservable. Writing it correctly is exactly what lets us estimate the wind. Second, we model wind as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process rather than white noise, because real gusts are correlated over seconds. Students often reach for an extended Kalman filter at this point, but note that the OU process is still linear, so the ordinary Kalman filter remains exactly optimal. We gain realism without losing optimality.
