An HMM hides a state. Ours hides one too — it is just continuous. Every algorithm carries over.

Viterbi too: with Gaussians, the smoothed answer is the most likely path.

This is the slide that ties the project to the syllabus, so spend time here. The point is that we are not doing something separate from HMMs — we are doing the same thing with a continuous state space. Instead of hidden states like Rainy and Sunny, our hidden state is a vector of nine real numbers. The forward algorithm becomes the Kalman filter. Forward-backward becomes the RTS smoother. Baum-Welch becomes EM for the noise covariances. Even the scaling factors have a counterpart: in the HMM we normalise alpha to stop underflow, and here we accumulate log-likelihood through the innovation covariance for exactly the same reason. And Viterbi: because everything is Gaussian and therefore has a single peak, the most likely path is just the sequence of smoothed means, so Viterbi and forward-backward agree here — which is not true for a discrete HMM.
