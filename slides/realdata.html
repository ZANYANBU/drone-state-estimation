Neither the sensor noise nor the wind is a number we chose.

The trajectory is simulated. The noise and the wind driving it are measurements.

We wanted to avoid the usual weakness of a simulation project, which is that every number is chosen by the author. So we measured two of them. First, the accelerometer noise comes from parsing a real PX4 flight log from an actual quadrotor - we measure the noise and the uncorrected bias from the log rather than assuming a value. Second, the turbulence: we pull a week of real hourly wind and gust observations from the Open-Meteo archive, find the gustiest hour, and use the gap between mean wind and gust to set the turbulence intensity. That hour had nearly ten metre per second gusts, which is genuinely rough flying. Everything then goes into a real SQLite database with a proper schema, about thirty-six thousand rows, and the results table on the next slide is produced by a SQL join against the ground truth, not by printing variables. We are honest that the trajectory itself is simulated; what is real is the noise and the weather driving it.
