Why this one?

Our model is linear. Its noise is Gaussian.
For that case Kalman is provably the best possible. Nothing can beat it.

Use the simplest method the problem allows.

We expect to be asked this, so here it is directly. The honest reason is that we built the model to be linear with Gaussian noise, and for that exact case there is a theorem: the Kalman filter is optimal. Not merely good or convenient — optimal. Nothing can beat it. So anything more sophisticated would be solving a harder problem than we actually have. Briefly down the list: averaging is meaningless because the sensors measure different quantities. A complementary filter takes two sensors with one hand-tuned weight and reports no uncertainty. Extended and unscented filters exist to handle non-linear models, and we went out of our way to keep ours linear, so they would only add approximation error. A particle filter costs roughly a hundred times more computation and only earns it when the uncertainty has several peaks, which ours does not. A discrete HMM cannot represent continuous position without millions of bins. And a neural network would need a large labelled dataset, report no uncertainty, and be unable to explain itself — and as our NIS finding shows, knowing the uncertainty is the entire point.
