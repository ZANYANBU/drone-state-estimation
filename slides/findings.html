Three things we did not expect

1

Accurate, but lying

The error looked fine. The filter’s own confidence was 5× too sure.

RMSE never showed it. The consistency test did.

2

It admits what it cannot know

Nothing measures sideways position — so that doubt grows forever.

Height stays pinned. That is honesty, not a bug.

3

Adding GPS made it worse

For a short flight it doubled the error. It only helps after ~5 minutes.

More sensors is not automatically better.

A filter can be right and still not know it.

These three findings turn a textbook exercise into a real investigation. The first is the most important. We validated with NEES and NIS, which check whether the filter's claimed uncertainty matches its actual error. The position error looked perfectly healthy at forty-three centimetres, but NIS came out at ten when it should be two - the filter was five times over-confident, because we had told it the optical flow noise was constant when in reality it grows with altitude. Scaling that noise with the estimated altitude fixed the consistency and barely moved the accuracy at all. If you only ever look at error, you never find this. Second, the uncertainty in sideways position grows forever because no sensor observes it, while height stays pinned by the altimeter - the mathematics telling us the truth about observability. Third, the counter-intuitive one: adding a GPS made things worse over a short flight, because optical flow was already more accurate than a consumer GPS. Fusion only helps when the new sensor beats what you already have.
