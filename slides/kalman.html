Six thousand times in sixty seconds. Sensors at different rates need no special handling.

Strip away the mathematics and the Kalman filter is two steps repeated. Predict: use physics to say where the drone should be a moment later. That guess always becomes less certain, because the world is not perfectly predictable. Correct: when a sensor reports, compare it with the prediction. The gap is called the innovation — more plainly, the surprise. Shift the guess toward the reading: a long way if that sensor is trustworthy, hardly at all if it is noisy, and the certainty improves. The important part is that the filter works out how far to move entirely by itself, from the noise levels. There are no hand-tuned weights anywhere. And because predicting is separate from correcting, sensors running at different rates need no special treatment: predict every step, correct only when something actually reports.
