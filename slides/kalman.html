Forward algorithm, continuous form

The Kalman filter

Predict

Run the physics forward. Uncertainty always grows.

x⁻ = A x + B u
P⁻ = A P Aᵀ + Q

The analogue of summing over the previous hidden state.

Update

Fold in whichever sensor just fired. Uncertainty shrinks.

y = z − H x⁻
S = H P⁻ Hᵀ + R
K = P⁻ Hᵀ S⁻¹

Bayes, exactly as in the HMM emission step.

Sensors arrive at different rates — so we predict every step and update only when a measurement exists. The gain K decides how far to trust each reading, and it converges to a steady value on its own.

Running log-likelihood from S is the continuous counterpart of Unit 4’s scaling factors.

The filter alternates two steps. Predict pushes the state forward through the dynamics and always inflates the covariance by Q, because the physics is uncertain. Update takes whichever sensor just reported, forms the innovation y — the surprise, how far the measurement is from what we expected — and corrects the state in proportion to the Kalman gain K. Note how naturally this handles multi-rate sensors: we predict every single step at 100 Hz, and we only update when a measurement actually exists. No interpolation, no resampling. And the log-likelihood we accumulate from the innovation covariance S is doing the same job as the scaling factors in the HMM forward pass: keeping the numbers numerically stable while giving us the probability of the whole observation sequence.
